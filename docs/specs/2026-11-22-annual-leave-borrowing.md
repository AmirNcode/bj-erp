# Annual leave borrowing

HR wants employees to use earned annual leave. Under Settings › Roles › Leave, an administrator can grant **Allow negative annual leave balance** to a role and assign it alongside the employee’s other roles. The permission starts off for everyone, including managers, HR and administrators. An explicit grant allows unlimited borrowing on the employee’s own daily or hourly annual leave. The usual approval chain still applies.

## Decisions

- Measure availability in minutes. Subtract other pending annual leave from the employee’s posted annual balance. Future accrual becomes available when posted. With three days earned and two pending, one more day fits.
- At submission and final approval, check the full charge against availability. At final approval exclude the request itself from pending time. Read the employee’s current permissions even when an administrator approves. A failed approval leaves the request pending and creates no consumption or unpaid portion.
- For an approved request edited to increase its charge, check only the extra minutes against availability. Allow reductions even when the employee is already below zero. Use the employee’s permission when HR makes the edit.
- Apply the final check to requests already pending when the policy takes effect. Preserve approved requests and ledger history, including existing negative balances. Monthly accrual continues to pay them down.
- Keep the existing sick-leave split, explicit unpaid leave, HR balance adjustments and conversion to unpaid. Borrowing continues to show the projected negative balance before the employee submits.

## Acceptance examples

| Case | Expected outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Manager or admin with three days available requests five | Submission is blocked |
| Employee with an explicitly authorized role requests five against three | May submit and, if approved, reaches −2 days |
| Employee loses the role while five days are pending and only three are available | Final approval is blocked. Posting two more days makes approval possible |
| Three days posted and two pending, HR increases another approved request by two | Increase is blocked. A one-day increase fits |
| Two simultaneous submissions each need the last available day | At most one is accepted for an employee without borrowing permission |
